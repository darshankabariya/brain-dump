1 · The Qubit & the Bloch Sphere

A classical bit is one of two points: 0 or 1. A qubit is any point on the surface of a sphere — |0⟩ at the north pole, |1⟩ at the south. The two angles below are the qubit's two real degrees of freedom: θ (latitude) sets the probabilities, φ (longitude) sets the phase. Gates are just rotations of this sphere.

Spin φ with θ fixed at 90° and the probabilities never change — yet the state does. That invisible-to-one-measurement phase is exactly what algorithms steer (see the Interference tab).

2 · Superposition & Measurement

Set how the qubit is split between |0⟩ and |1⟩, then measure. Each measurement returns a single classical bit and collapses the state — the superposition is destroyed. Measure many times (re-preparing each time) and the tally converges to the Born-rule probabilities |α|² and |β|². You can put a continuum in; you only ever get one bit out.

Before measuring, the qubit is genuinely both at once. After, it's a plain classical bit and stays that way — measure again, same answer. This one-shot, destructive read is why quantum algorithms must arrange the answer before looking.

3 · Interference — the engine

Amplitudes are arrows (complex numbers), not just probabilities. Two paths leading to the same outcome add as arrows. Point them together → constructive (big probability). Point them apart → destructive (they cancel, probability → 0). Probabilities alone can never cancel — this is the one thing a quantum computer has that a classical random machine doesn't.

sum amplitude |A+B|—
probability |A+B|²—

This is literally the double-slit experiment: each "path" is a slit, the screen brightness is |A+B|². Set the phases 180° apart and the spot goes dark even though each path alone would light it up. Grover and Shor are elaborate versions of exactly this.

4 · Entanglement — the Bell pair

Build the Bell state (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2 with two gates: H on the top qubit, then CNOT. Afterwards each qubit alone is a fair coin, but the two coins always agree — outcomes 01 and 10 never happen. That correlation has no "they had answers all along" explanation (Bell's theorem).

Watch 01 and 10 stay at zero. Each single qubit is 50/50 random, yet the pair is locked together. No information travels between them — each side alone sees pure noise — so there's no faster-than-light signalling.

5 · Gates & Circuits — build one

6 · Grover's Search — amplitude amplification

Find the one marked item among 16 with no structure to exploit. Classically that's ~N/2 guesses. Grover does it in ~√N steps by repeating: (1) oracle flips the marked item's phase, (2) diffusion reflects all amplitudes about their average — nudging the whole state toward the answer. Watch the marked bar grow… and shrink again if you over-iterate (interference cuts both ways).
